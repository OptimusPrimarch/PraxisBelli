# Faction & Weapon Identity — working draft

> Proposal for reaction, not a locked design. Everything here is reversible.

---

## The premise: magic is sworn, not cast

**In this world, power comes from binding obligation.** An oath spoken and kept channels force into the speaker; an oath broken inverts and channels something worse. There are no wizards. There are people who have promised things, and the world holds them to it.

This is the arcanepunk hook, and it does three jobs at once:

1. It's **not 40k's** — the Imperium's faith is devotional, this is contractual. Closer to fae bargains or Norse oath-rings than to the God-Emperor.
2. It **explains the industrial layer.** Sworn power is scarce and personal, so mass warfare still runs on powder, steam, and artillery. Arcanepunk rather than high fantasy.
3. It **falls straight out of the faction names you already chose.** Oathkeepers, Oathbreakers, Saints, Regiments were sitting there implying a cosmology.

---

## The Sworn Empire

**The Empire is not a state that uses oath-magic. The Empire *is* the oath-network.**

Every subject holds an oath to someone above them, who holds one above them, up to a binding at the centre that nobody living has read. Citizenship is not a birthright, it is a *contract position*. This is why the Empire coheres across impossible distance without instant communication: the oaths hold whether or not anyone is watching, and a province that breaks faith stops receiving power in a way that is immediately, physically obvious.

It also explains the Empire's cruelty without needing a cartoon tyrant. **The oath-network is load-bearing.** One province breaking faith weakens every oath chained beneath it. The Empire persecutes oathbreaking the way a body fights sepsis — not out of malice, but because the alternative is structural collapse. Everyone in the Empire is complicit in something they can feel the necessity of.

### The Imperial factions — four tiers of binding

| Faction | Analogue | Oath tier | Material posture |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Imperial Regiments** | Astra Militarum | **The Levy Oath** — shallow, brief, sworn by the thousand and expiring on discharge. Grants nothing; costs everything. | Powder, steam, artillery, bodies |
| **Imperial Forgesworn** | Mechanicus | **The Binding Oath** — sworn *to* things rather than people. They put oaths *into* machines. | Engines, constructs, warded plate |
| **Imperial Saints** | Sisters of Battle | **The Choral Oath** — sworn as a body, sustained by voice. Strong while the choir holds. | Sacred flame, censer, banner |
| **Imperial Oathkeepers** | Space Marines | **The Deep Oath** — so total it reshapes the swearer. Cannot be released, only broken. | Warded plate, few, irreplaceable |

**Imperial Forgesworn** are the keystone the roster was missing. If oaths are contracts, someone had to work out how to swear one *to an object* — and that discovery is what industrialises the setting. They forge the Oathkeepers' warded plate, bind the engines that move Regiment armor, and maintain the oath-infrastructure the Empire physically runs on. Every other Imperial faction is downstream of them. Mechanically they should be the **construct faction**: units that have no Mettle of their own because the oath is in the chassis, not the crew.

**Oathbreaker Legions** sit outside the tiers — the Deep Oath inverted. They and the Oathkeepers are **the same order, before and after**: same armor patterns, same weapons, same silhouettes, one set maintained and one set festering. Shared chassis with inverted trait packages is cheap to build and reads instantly across the table.

## The First Binding, and the Unbinding

**The plane itself is held by an oath.** Sworn at the beginning, by parties unrecorded, on terms nobody now living has read — the **First Binding**. Matter holds its shape, cause precedes effect, and the dead stay dead because something promised they would.

**"Chaos," here, is simply the breaking of that oath.** Not a pantheon, not a rival power — a *breach of contract* at the scale of reality. The **Unbinding** is what leaks through where the First Binding has failed, and the **Forsworn** are everything that serves it.

This does something the rest of the setting was reaching for and hadn't yet grasped: **it collapses every scale of oathbreaking into one act.** A deserter abandoning his levy oath, a magistrate taking a bribe, a legion turning on its Empire, and a daemon clawing through a thin place are *the same event at different magnitudes*. Each one is a crack in the same structure.

Which means the Empire's merciless persecution of oathbreaking was never metaphor. The sepsis line is literal. A province that breaks faith is not merely disloyal — it is **structurally load-bearing masonry coming away from reality.** The Empire is monstrous, and the Empire is correct, and it cannot afford for anyone to notice the difference.

### What a daemon is

**A daemon is what a promise becomes when it is broken and has nowhere to go.**

Oaths are real objects in this world. Break one and the binding doesn't evaporate — it is left with force and no object, obligation with nothing to obligate. It goes looking. The oldest and hungriest of them have been looking a very long time, and they are drawn to the act that made them: they gather wherever someone is *about* to break faith, the way flies gather before a wound.

This is why the Forsworn are not recruited so much as *accumulated*. Nobody joins them. People simply break something, and find they have company.

### Unbound space

Where the First Binding is thin, the rules of the plane are merely *conventional* rather than enforced. Distance stops being reliable — which is precisely why the Empire's ships can cross it faster than light should allow, and precisely why doing so is an act of calculated heresy every single time. **Imperial logistics run on the wound.** The Forgesworn know this better than anyone, which sharpens the open question of whether they are the Empire's foundation or its greatest heresy.

### The design principle this hands you

The Forsworn should be **the faction whose rules break the rulebook's own contract.**

Every other faction obeys the stated structure: alternate activations, one activation per unit per round, suppression clears at end of round. The Forsworn are thematically the violation of exactly that kind of agreement — so their signature rules should be the ones that *break stated rules elsewhere in the book*. `Corrupt` already does this in miniature: it is the one suppression in the game that does not clear.

Powerful, and worth rationing hard. One or two genuine rule-breaks per faction, each one load-bearing and named, is a faction identity. Five is an unplayable mess.

> **Naming note:** *Oathbreaker Legions* remain the human legions among the Forsworn — the Deep Oath inverted. They are the faction you can field. **The Unbinding** is the cosmic principle behind them, and probably never gets a stat line at all.

## The Xenos — outside the network

The Oath is a *human* technology. That's the whole xenos design space: every alien faction is defined by **how it fails to fit the network**, which makes each one a different attack on the rules the Imperial factions take for granted.

> All four are stubs. Named, hooked, and mechanically pointed — nothing statted.

| Faction | Analogue | Relationship to the Oath | Mechanical hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Unspoken** | Tyranids | **Cannot swear.** No voice, no self, nothing to bind. | **No Mettle stat at all.** Immune to suppression and the entire morale track. The faction that ignores half your rulebook. |
| **The Interred** | Necrons | **An oath that outlived its purpose.** Still executing a promise whose swearer is dust and whose terms nobody remembers. | Slow, immensely tough, and **returns** — models that come back because the contract isn't finished. |
| **The Thornbound** | Aeldari | **Loophole masters.** They swear freely and mean none of it; every oath has an escape clause. | Fast, fragile, conditional. Rules that trigger on *breaking* an agreement — feints, redeployment, withdrawal. |
| **Scrapjaw Hordes** | Orks | **The oath is a joke.** They'll swear anything, cheerfully, and forget by morning. | Cheap and numerous, and **never Rout** — not from courage, but because there's nothing in them to break. |

The Unspoken and the Scrapjaws both ignore morale, but for opposite reasons and with opposite feels: one is an unfeeling tide, the other is delighted. Worth keeping distinct — the Unspoken should be *frightening*, the Scrapjaws *exhausting*.

There's a live worldbuilding hook here worth not resolving yet: **the Interred's forgotten oath is probably still binding on someone.** That's a campaign, not a stat line.

## The proxy contract

**Design rule, binding on everything below:** anyone with an existing collection should be able to sit down and play, and anyone starting fresh should never be steered toward one manufacturer.

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **Unit sizes are 5, 8, 10, or 16** | 5 and 10 are the near-universal box counts across 40k, Warmachine, Konflikt '47, and Marcher. 8 and 16 fall out of 2×/2× on those. |
| **No base-size requirements, ever** | True line of sight already governs targeting. A rule that says "32mm base" makes half the world's minis illegal. Measure model-to-model. |
| **Archetype names, never proper nouns** | "Line Infantry," "Heavy Weapons Team," "Armored Fighting Vehicle." Never a name that only fits one company's fiction. |
| **Weapons named by function** | "Heavy Machine Gun," not "Heavy Bolter." A player should be able to point at any model's gun and know which profile it is. |
| **Every faction fields a recognizable silhouette set** | Armored elites, massed infantry, a tank, a walker, a flyer. If a faction needs a model shape nobody makes, it's a bad faction. |

The fiction is distinct; the **shapes** are deliberately conventional. That's the whole trick.

## Mechanical faction identity

### The design rule

**One signature trait per faction.** Everything else — the weapon list, the equipment, the category caps, the doctrine — exists to *reinforce that one idea*. Identity comes from the combination, never from a pile of special rules.

This is deliberate, and it follows from where balance risk actually lives:

> The points formula prices Speed, Mettle, Evasion, Armor, and Toughness from real probability curves, so **stat divergence is nearly free** — it changes texture, not advantage. Traits are hand-priced guesses. So: **be generous with stats, stingy with traits.**

Each faction also owns **one characteristic decision** it makes every turn that nobody else makes. That's what makes factions feel different once the numbers are balanced by construction.

### The elite constraint — and its limit

Elite factions field half the models. Durability prices correctly, but **output does not** — 5 rifles is half of 10 rifles, and the formula won't flag it because each rifle is priced fairly in isolation.

The fix is **better weapons per model**, and that is all. Both durability and armament are things the points formula already prices honestly, so an elite squad carrying A2/AP2 weapons where line infantry carries A1/AP1 is a *priced* solution, not a compensation mechanic.

> **The game is balanced around the rifle squad, not around elites** (see design bible, Principle 0). Elites are the deviation. When a five-model squad seems to need a new subsystem to be viable, the correct response is to fix its stat line or its price — not to add a rule. An elite unit's cost *is* its downside, and it is allowed to have one.

This is a live risk on this project, not a hypothetical: designing around Oathkeeper problems produced a pass-token rule, a transport-slot rule, and a three-axis compensation doctrine in the space of one conversation. Each was defensible alone. The accumulation was not.

**What survived that audit, and why** — each earns its place for line infantry independently:

- **Pass Tokens granting a half-Move** — applies to *any* outnumbered list. A Regiments player facing a horde army is outnumbered too. Symmetric, one sentence, no new resource.
- **Dedicated Transports** — a Regiments player wants their APC out of the SUPPORT slot just as much as an Oathkeeper does. Standard practice, and Marcher precedent.
- **Hull classes** — vehicle design space for everyone; Regiments simply favour the Bus.

**What was cut:** "stronger signature rules" as a third compensation axis for elites. Stats and weapons only.

### Imperial Regiments — *weight of materiel*

> **The decision:** *do I spend bodies?* Casualties are a resource; position is what you buy with them.

- **Signature trait — Massed Ranks:** while this unit contains 8 or more models, it re-rolls to-hit results of 1 and gains +1 Mettle. Strength is literally in numbers, and it drains away as they die.
- **Stat shape:** Mettle 2–4, Armor 3–5, Toughness 1 throughout. Cheapest per model, largest units in the game.
- **Reinforcing tools:** the **Surplus Rifle** (Levy's issue — carbine range, rifle stats otherwise, deliberately unremarkable rather than deliberately terrible), Suppressing machine guns, and now a full Mortar/MLRS indirect-fire tier. Volume over quality at every tier. *(A "Barrage" artillery trait — fire without LOS or a Spotter — was floated here early on but never actually built; `Indirect` still requires a Spotter for everyone, Regiments included.)*
- **Doctrine — *Fix Bayonets*:** for the rest of the round, LINE units in this Platoon ignore the Frozen state.

### Imperial Forgesworn — *the bound engine*

> **The decision:** *what do I commit?* Constructs cannot be rallied or redirected — once pointed, they go.

- **Signature trait — Bound Spirit:** this unit has no Mettle characteristic. It automatically passes Mettle checks and ignores suppression markers entirely, but cannot benefit from Leadership Aura or any effect that would modify Mettle. The oath is in the chassis, not the crew.
- **Stat shape:** high Armor (6–8), Toughness 3–10, Speed low. No Mettle at all. Small unit counts.
- **Reinforcing tools:** **Hardpoints** as standard (ignore Heavy), precise long-range weapons, walker and construct chassis. They never panic and never hurry.
- **Doctrine — *Sealed Orders*:** for the rest of the round, units in this Platoon may not be targeted by enemy Command abilities and ignore Terrifying.

### Imperial Oathkeepers — *the unbroken line*

> **The decision:** *which unit eats the hit?* Oathbound only holds while unwounded, so the first casualty on any squad is a real loss of capability.

- **Signature trait — Oathbound:** while this unit has suffered no wounds, attacks against it are made at −1 AP. It automatically passes its first Mettle check each game. If it would ever Rout, it is removed from play instead — an Oathkeeper does not flee.
- **Stat shape:** Armor 7–8, Mettle 6–7, **Toughness 2**, units of 5. Most expensive per model in the game.
- **Reinforcing tools:** genuinely better guns — A2/AP2 where Regiments carry A1/AP1 — plus power weapons at squad level. They must out-shoot per model to justify half the model count.
- **Doctrine — *Hold the Oath*:** for the rest of the round, units in this Platoon cannot be moved or placed by enemy effects, and ignore Suppressing.

### Imperial Saints — *the choir*

> **The decision:** *do I hold the choir together?* Hymnal wants 6" coherence; objectives want you spread across the board. That tension is the faction.

- **Signature trait — Hymnal:** while within 6" of another friendly unit with Hymnal, this unit gains +1 Mettle. The choir builds itself up and comes apart as it is picked off.
- **Stat shape:** Armor 5–6, **highest Mettle in the game** (7–8), units of 8–10. Middling durability, near-unbreakable resolve.
- **Reinforcing tools:** **Sacred Flame** weapons (hits inflict a suppression marker in addition to damage, and ignore the Cover tag), censers, banners. Short-ranged and punishing — the choir has to close.
- **Doctrine — *Act of Faith*:** for the rest of the round, units in this Platoon automatically pass every Mettle check, but each unit that does so takes 1 wound at end of round.

### Oathbreaker Legions — *the debt comes due*

> **The decision:** *how much do I pay?* Blood Debt makes damage taken into damage dealt, so every casualty is also an argument for pushing further in.

- **Signature trait — Blood Debt:** this unit gains +1 AP for each wound it has suffered this round, to a maximum of +3. It becomes more dangerous as it dies.
- **Stat shape:** the Oathkeeper chassis — Armor 7, Toughness 2, units of 5 — but **erratic Mettle** (3–5) and higher Damage weapons. Violence bought with reliability.
- **Reinforcing tools:** **Corrupt** weapons (suppression markers that do *not* clear at end of round — the only lingering suppression in the game), unstable high-Damage guns, MONSTER-type units at LINE.
- **Doctrine — *Break the Chains*:** for the rest of the round, units in this Platoon gain +2 Speed and may charge after Running, but automatically fail their next Mettle check.

### Hard category caps

Each faction **overrides** the generic Vanguard spread. This is the cheapest identity lever in the game — no new rules, fully enforceable in NewRecruit, and it shapes what a list even looks like before a die is rolled.

**Every faction can reach every category.** Emphasis is about *how much*, never *whether* — a hard zero would break the proxy contract, since a player with an existing collection could discover their faction structurally cannot field models they already own.

| Faction | COMMAND | LINE | RECON | SHOCK | ARMOR | SUPPORT |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Generic (Vanguard)* | 1 | 2–4 | 0–2 | 0–2 | 0–2 | 0–2 |
| **Regiments** | 1 | **2–5** | 0–2 | 0–1 | **0–3** | **0–3** |
| **Forgesworn** | 1 | 1–3 | 0–2 | 0–1 | **0–3** | **0–4** |
| **Oathkeepers** | 1 | 1–4 | 0–1 | **0–3** | 0–1 | 0–1 |
| **Saints** | **1–2** | 2–4 | 0–1 | 0–2 | 0–1 | 0–2 |
| **Oathbreakers** | 1 | 1–3 | 0–1 | **0–4** | 0–2 | 0–1 |

Saints are the only faction fielding two COMMAND. Oathbreakers reach four SHOCK, Forgesworn four SUPPORT, Regiments five LINE — each faction has exactly one category it can push further than anyone else.

## Combined-arms character & staples

Every faction fields a complete combined-arms force. What differs is **which arm carries the weight, and what the others are doing while it does.**

A faction's **staple** is the unit that expresses its answer to the combined-arms problem — the thing it always brings, because without it the rest of the list doesn't function.

### Armor doctrine by hull class

Because a Dedicated Transport takes its payload's CATEGORY and does not spend an extra slot, **which hull class a faction favours is its armor doctrine** — and it explains why two factions with identical ARMOR caps can feel nothing alike.

| Faction | Hull class | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Regiments** | **Bus** | High capacity, token guns, cheap. Armor and transport are *separate* things — they field pure tanks in ARMOR 0–3 and buses to move mass. The conventional army. |
| **Forgesworn** | **Gunboat**, rarely | Constructs mostly walk. What transports they field are heavily armed and slow. |
| **Oathkeepers** | **Gunboat** | The defining choice. Capped at ARMOR 0–1, so nearly every vehicle they field is *also* carrying a squad. They run few tanks in the conventional sense because most of their tanks are transports. |
| **Saints** | **Assault Transport** | Their doctrine question is "can I close?" — so they buy hulls that survive the approach and disgorge into melee. |
| **Oathbreakers** | **Assault Transport** | Same answer, worse patience. Fast, fragile, committed. |

Oathkeepers are the clearest case: a low ARMOR cap does not make them armor-poor, it makes their armor *carry infantry*. Their tanks and their squads advance together because they are physically the same choice — combined arms enforced by list construction rather than by a rule telling them to cooperate.

### Imperial Regiments — *coordination*

**Staple: the Rifle Squad.** Ten bodies, cheap, unremarkable alone.

The complete classical army, and therefore **the teaching faction**: infantry hold, armor breaks through, artillery kills, recon sees for it. Every arm is individually mediocre and the whole is devastating. A Regiments player learns the game's dependencies because none of their units can cover for a missing one.

### Imperial Regiments — unit roster, naming, and the Wehrmacht-as-template lens

The rest of this document was written before the Regiments roster got built out in detail. That work used a specific lens worth recording here, not just in the roster tables elsewhere: **real-world Wehrmacht force structure as a template for role specialization and chassis reuse — explicitly not for ideology, and not for exact unit names.** The two things worth borrowing were the organizational logic (infantry role specialization like Pioniere/Jäger, and building several vehicles off one proven chassis) and nothing else.

**Naming runs on two deliberately contrasting themes** — machines get predator nicknames (what the factory built), soldiers get martial titles (what the person earned or was assigned):

- **Vehicle chassis — Mustelidae** (the weasel family), chosen over Canidae (wolf/fox/jackal/coyote are all real, currently-fielded vehicle names already — picking them would read as "didn't check") and over Bears (only ~8 species, no size gradient — every bear reads "big and tough," which can't differentiate a light scout chassis from a heavy gun chassis by name alone). Mustelids uniquely offer both: a genuine size gradient (the smallest carnivore in the world up to something that fights bears) and an almost entirely unclaimed naming pool. Vehicles are named individually per unit sharing a chassis stat line (the StuG/Hetzer/Marder precedent — distinct proper names, not a swap-a-turret mechanic), not as "Model + suffix."
  - **Light chassis** (Spd9/Met4/Eva6/Arm5/Tuf6): **Stoat** (scout bus), **Weasel** (recon car), **Mink** (light gunboat)
  - **Medium chassis** (Spd8/Met4/Eva5/Arm6/Tuf9): **Marten** (bus), **Sable** (gunboat), **Fisher** (tank-gun turret), **Tayra** (self-propelled gun)
  - **Wolverine** — the bespoke Laser Cannon specialist, deliberately *not* part of either family (apex, solitary — "we built exactly one of these")
  - Reserved, not yet spent: **Badger** (dig behavior — earmarked for a future Sapper engineering vehicle), **Otter** (earmarked for a future amphibious variant)
- **Infantry — martial titles**, not another animal family. Explored and rejected as full alternate themes: birds/raptors (individual fits were strong — Caracara for "generalist/flexible" was excellent — but mixing one animal name into an otherwise all-martial-vocabulary roster read as the odd one out), industrial tools, weather, insect castes, geology (reserved for Forgesworn instead — don't spend materials imagery on Regiments).

|---|---|---|

| **Fusilier** | Rifle Squad | The anchor |
| **Grenadier** | Veteran Squad | Historically *was* the elite/veteran designation in most 18th–19th century armies |
| **Vanguard** | Storm Squad | — |

#### Weapon tiers

Reorganized by what carries the weapon, not what it's for — see `reference.html` §13 for the full current library. **Tier 1 Infantry** (man-portable), **Tier 2 Crew-served** (small team, or a vehicle's secondary/hull mount), **Tier 3 Vehicular/Towable** (needs a hull or a carriage). This is also where **Mortar**, **MLRS**, **Heavy Autocannon**, and **Recoilless Rifle** got added — the tier framing surfaced that mortars and MLRS were genuine gaps, not just missing flavor.

#### Dragoon — Rapid Deployment

The unit that actually proves "INF+vehicle synergy" rather than just asserting it. Two iterations, the first one rejected for a reason worth remembering:

1. **Rejected: free/guaranteed re-embark after shooting.** Broken because the game has no reaction economy — a unit that shoots and hides entirely within its own activation is *never* exposed to return fire, which is strictly stronger than anything else gets to be.
2. **Kept: free *dismount* instead.** When the Transport carrying this unit activates, it may order the unit to disembark as part of that activation — no cost to the Transport's actions, no cost to the Dragoons' Move stat or either of their own two actions. When the Dragoons later take their own activation, both actions are fully theirs. This fixes the exposure problem structurally: the moment they act, they're an ordinary target until they spend a full activation getting back to safety.

Balance is deliberately **structural, not probabilistic** — no Mettle check, no dice. The tax is that the ability requires a *live* Transport (kill it, Rapid Deployment is gone for the rest of the game) and that the player is paying for two units to get one effect. Hard restrictions: works with **any** friendly Transport, not a specific chassis; **no Heavy-trait weapons** (directly contradicts "hit hard and fast").

This also produced a new **general** hull rule (not Dragoon-specific — lives on the hull, so any unit riding one benefits): a Transport with **token** armament (Bus) must choose between firing and ordering a disembark in the same activation; a Transport with **real** armament (Gunboat, Assault Transport) may do both — the real-world IFV/APC distinction (a Bradley fights while delivering troops, an M113 doesn't) mapped exactly onto the Bus/Gunboat split that already existed, so it needed no new concept.

Command Points were floated as a natural extension of the fire-or-deliver choice ("ripe territory for later") and explicitly rejected once reminded that §8 already states "No resource economy. Confirmed, not deferred." If a spendable-resource want resurfaces, extend Platoon Abilities rather than inventing a parallel system.

### Imperial Forgesworn — *overmatch*

**Staple: the walker.** A construct that is simultaneously their armor and their fire support.

Firepower-led. SUPPORT to four and ARMOR to three means they out-range and out-shoot anyone, but they are **slow and cannot hold what they kill** — Bound Spirit constructs never panic and never hurry. Their infantry exists to sit on objectives while the guns do the work. The combined-arms question they must answer every game: *can my guns kill it before it reaches the thing I can't afford to lose?*

### Imperial Oathkeepers — *compression*

**Staple: the five-model Oathkeeper squad.** Infantry that does three jobs.

Their infantry is durable enough to serve as armor, and armed well enough to serve as fire support. They compress the triangle rather than spreading it — LINE 1–4 and SHOCK 0–3 with almost nothing elsewhere. The tradeoff is real: **very few activations**, so every one must count, and losing a single squad is losing a quarter of the army's capability.

### Imperial Saints — *convergence*

**Staple: the flame squad.** Short-ranged, punishing, and useless at distance.

Close-support-led. Hymnal's 6" coherence means they fight as a **converging mass** rather than a dispersed line — the faction that physically clusters. They can field recon and armor but rarely want to, because anything that leaves the choir stops benefiting from it. Their combined arms is *mutual support between infantry*, and their question is: *can I close before the shooting phase kills me?*

### Oathbreaker Legions — *tempo*

**Staple: the shock squad, or the monster.** Whatever arrives first and hurts most.

Assault-led, SHOCK to four. They don't combine arms so much as **collapse the game before the opponent's arms can cooperate** — kill the RECON and the artillery goes blind, kill the COMMAND and the Mettle collapses. Blood Debt means their casualties fund the push. Their question is the harshest: *if this doesn't break them this round, can I survive next round?*

### Trait pricing (first-draft multipliers)

Faction traits are hand-priced like every other trait, applied to the unit total:

| Trait | Multiplier | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Massed Ranks | ×1.06 | Conditional on 8+ models, and decays as they die |
| Bound Spirit | ×1.10 | Immunity to a whole subsystem, offset by losing Leadership Aura |
| Oathbound | ×1.15 | −1 AP is large; the Rout clause is a real drawback in a losing game |
| Hymnal | ×1.08 | Strong but positionally expensive |
| Blood Debt | ×1.12 | Up to +3 AP, but only by taking damage |

> These are **guesses**, flagged as such. They're the least-defensible numbers in the whole system and the first thing playtesting should attack.

## Weapon identity — what each tier is *for*

The current formula prices weapons accurately but the roster doesn't yet make them feel different. Proposed tiers:

| Tier | Shape | Role | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sidearm** | A2, AP0, D1, 8" | Exists to be usable while engaged | Pistol |
| **Standard** | A1, AP1, D1, 18" | The baseline every line unit carries | Rifle |
| **Chaff** | A1, AP0, D1, 10–12" | Unremarkable rather than deliberately bad; makes hordes affordable | Autogun, Surplus Rifle |
| **Automatic** | A4, AP1–3, D1–2, 18–24" | Volume + Suppressing. Anti-infantry | Machine guns |
| **Special** | A1, AP4+, D2, 18" | Squad-portable answer to armor | Plasma Rifle |
| **Obliterator** | A1, AP7+, **D6+**, 30"+ | One shot, one kill. Must threaten Toughness 10 | Laser Cannon |
| **Ordnance** | A1–2, AP4–6, D4–7, 30"+, Blast | Area denial, indirect | Main Cannon, Howitzer |

**The obliterator rule of thumb:** a dedicated anti-armor weapon needs `Damage ≥ 6` to be worth its slot, because the heaviest chassis sit at Toughness 10. At D3 it is not an anti-tank gun, it is a rifle with good AP — which is exactly why the Laser Cannon mispriced at 17 points before its Damage was raised.

## Roadmap

Ordered by what unblocks the most downstream work.

### Built
- ✅ **Imperial Regiments** — 11 units, all six categories, formula-priced, live in NewRecruit.

### Next — Imperial, to prove the axes work
1. **Imperial Oathkeepers** — the elite end of the curve. Proves Toughness 2, small units, and the single-model premium behave. Biggest stress test the points formula will get.

## Open questions

1. Does the **Oath** premise land, or is it too tidy? It's load-bearing for every faction now, Imperial and xenos alike — cheap to reject today, expensive later.
2. **Oathkeepers/Oathbreakers as one order split in two** — good design economy, or does it make Oathbreakers feel derivative rather than their own faction?
3. **Toughness 2 on elite infantry.** The single biggest lever for making Oathkeepers feel unlike Regiments. Doubles effective durability before Armor applies — needs checking against the points curve before it's baked in.
4. Should **Category access limits** be hard caps, or soft (costed) restrictions?
5. ~~`Conscript` giving a flat −20% is the only trait that touches points directly.~~ **Resolved and superseded.** `Conscript` is −25%, not −20%, and it's no longer the only points-touching trait — there's now a full system of it (weapon-trait tiers, faction signature-trait multipliers, and flat ability tiers; see design-bible.md §11 steps 3 and 5).
6. **Do the Forgesworn have their own agenda?** A Mechanicus-equivalent that is merely loyal is wasted. They hold the only oaths sworn to *things*, and they run the logistics that cross unbound space — which makes them either the Empire's foundation or its greatest heresy, and they'd know which.
7. **Is the Empire's central binding the same oath as the First Binding?** If yes, the Empire is literally the institution holding reality together, and every atrocity is arguably justified. If no, the Empire is a smaller thing wearing the authority of a larger one. This is the single biggest lever on the setting's tone.
8. **Can a broken oath be repaired, or only replaced?** Decides whether redemption exists as a story. Also decides whether an Oathbreaker Legion can ever become Oathkeepers again — a campaign hook either way.
9. **How much does the Empire know about daemon-formation?** If they know daemons are made from broken promises, then every execution for oathbreaking is also a containment procedure, and the Saints' role changes from zealotry to sanitation.
