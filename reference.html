Principle Zero

This game is balanced around a regular rifleman squad. Not around elites.

Ten bodies, Toughness 1, Armor 4, Evasion 6, Speed 5, Mettle 4, 100 points. That is the reference unit, the formula's calibration anchor, and the mental model for army size — a 1000-point list is about ten rifle squads' worth of stuff.

Three anti-spiral rules follow:

  1. Every mechanic is judged by what it does to a rifle squad. If a rule is neutral or confusing for line infantry and only makes sense for a five-model elite squad, it is the wrong rule.
  2. When an elite unit needs a new subsystem to function, the elite is wrong — not the game. Elites get better stats and better weapons, both of which the points formula already prices. They do not get compensation mechanics.
  3. New systems must justify themselves for the common case. A rule introduced to solve an elite faction's problem must also earn its place in a mirror match of line infantry.

This guards against a failure mode that already occurred once on this project: designing around one faction's problems produced three new subsystems in a single sitting. Each was defensible alone; the accumulation was not.

The proxy contract

Binding on all content: anyone with an existing collection should be able to sit down and play, and anyone starting fresh should never be steered toward one manufacturer.

Weapons named by function"Heavy Machine Gun," not a setting-specific brand name.
Buildable silhouettesIf a faction needs a model shape nobody manufactures, it is a bad faction.
01

The Dice Engine

Every check in the game is roll-over on a d10: meet or exceed the target number to succeed. There is no roll-under anywhere.

An unmodified 1 always fails. An unmodified 10 always succeeds, except against Armor above 10 (below).

The attack sequence

Attacks resolve in two stages, and the attacker rolls both.

  1. To-hit — roll a d10 per attack die against the target's Evasion. Total dice = the weapon's Attacks × models firing it.
  2. Damage check — each hit rolls again against the target's Armor, after AP is subtracted. Each success inflicts the weapon's Damage in wounds against the target's Toughness.

Higher Evasion is harder to hit; higher Armor is harder to damage. There is no attacker-side accuracy stat — to-hit difficulty is entirely defender-side, and attacker differentiation comes from traits, re-rolls, and Anti-[Keyword] weapons.

Armor above 10

Armor is not capped at 10. When a target's Armor after AP exceeds 10, an unmodified 10 no longer succeeds automatically — it buys a second roll against Armor − 10, cascading again if that also exceeds 10.

A hull at Armor 17 struck by an AP 0 weapon needs a 10, then a 7+: a 4% chance. Vanishingly unlikely, never impossible, and the curve continues smoothly instead of hitting a wall. This makes AP the only real answer to heavy armor — that same hull struck at AP 6 rolls against an effective 11, more than twice as likely to wound.

The AP scale

AP is calibrated against real anti-armor performance, so weapon design has an intuitive reference.

Most infantry weapons sit at AP 0. Penetration is bought through heavy weapon teams and vehicles, not carried by line infantry — a large part of why combined arms is mandatory rather than merely encouraged.

Range bands

Range is a single Short value on the profile. Medium = 2× that distance at +1 to the target's Evasion. Long = 3× at +3. Accurate ignores all range penalties.

02

Activation & Actions

Alternating activation, unit by unit. The round ends once every unit has activated.

2 actions per activation, taken in any order, each used once per activation. There is no general reaction economy; triggered abilities are written per-unit as named exceptions.

Actions

MoveMove the leader model by its Speed, then re-form (§03).
ShootAttack with a ranged weapon.
FightAttack with a melee weapon, or unarmed (§04).
RallyRemove all suppression markers from this unit.
ClaimTake an objective. LINE units do this for free.

Pass Tokens

Only the player with fewer units receives them, equal to the delta between the two unit counts. No other source of passes.

Spending a Pass Token lets one friendly unit that has not yet activated make a free half-Move. That unit is not considered activated and may still act normally later in the round.

An outnumbered army's real deficit is not tempo — pass tokens already keep the round even in count — it is board coverage. Six units cannot be in as many places as twelve, and since only infantry can claim objectives, being outnumbered means being out-positioned. Repositioning is the exact compensation the deficit calls for. A Pass Token buys movement and nothing else; it is deliberately not a resource economy.

03

Movement & Formation

No coherency stat. Move the leader model by its Speed, then place the rest of the unit within 2" of the leader, or within 2" of two models that are each within 2" of the leader. Formation re-forms every time the unit moves.

Engagement range is 1" baseline, extendable by weapon or trait.

True line of sight throughout — base size implies a volume.

04

Melee

Fire into melee is allowed. A missed shot against an engaged target instead hits an ally in that fight — the opponent chooses which model eats the miss.

Fire out of melee is not allowed, except Pistol, which may make a ranged attack while its bearer is engaged, but only against an enemy unit it is engaged with.

Fists

Every model carries this weapon profile whether or not it also has a melee weapon — written up as a real profile rather than an abstract rule, so anything referencing "a melee weapon" resolves without a special case.

Fists · Melee · A1 · AP0 · D1 · Fixed, Unarmed
Unarmed — this weapon's target has its Evasion and Armor each increased by 1 for that attack.

Nothing is ever helpless in melee — it is simply bad at it. Against a baseline target Fists are about 69% as effective as a Bayonet, so a real melee weapon is a genuine upgrade rather than the difference between fighting and not.

Pricing consequence: because every model has Fists, it is free, by the same logic that makes TYPE and CATEGORY bundles free. Real melee weapons are priced at their margin over Fists, not their absolute value.

05

Morale

The Mettle check

Roll d10 + Mettle. Pass if the total ≥ 10 + suppression markers held.

Higher Mettle is better, like every other stat on the card. A unit with Mettle 4 holding two markers needs an 8+.

  • Pass — remove one suppression marker.
  • Fail — step up one level on the track.

Check timing: at the start of a unit's activation if it holds any markers, or immediately when an effect forces one.

Suppression markers

Markers persist until removed. They do not clear at end of round.

MethodRemoves
Passing a Mettle check1 marker
The Rally actionAll markers on this unit
Specific effects (Hold Fast)As written

Recovery

A unit steps down one level at the end of its activation only if it holds no suppression markers.

This single clause is what makes the morale system function. With automatic recovery, a unit checked at the start of its activation, went Shaken, acted, and recovered at the end of that same activation — but Shaken's penalties only bite when being attacked, during the opponent's activations. The defensive half of the penalty could essentially never apply, and Frozen was unreachable. The whole track was ornamental.

What it creates instead is a genuine decision every activation:

  • Rally — spend half your activation clearing markers, recover at end of turn.
  • Don't — take both actions, keep the markers, stay Shaken through the opponent's turn at −1 Evasion / −1 Armor, and check again next activation at the same penalty. Fail twice and you are Frozen.

The real price of suppression is actions, not damage — which is also what justifies Suppressing costing points.

The track

LevelEffect
DeterminedBaseline.
Shaken−1 Evasion, −1 Armor, −1 Mettle. The whole card degrades uniformly.
FrozenShaken's penalties, and no Move actions. Can still Shoot and Fight.
RoutingMust spend its entire activation moving toward the nearest board edge. Reaching it removes the unit from play.

Vehicles run the same four steps but end in Destroyed rather than Routing.

06

Terrain

Terrain is a set of composable tags. Any piece can carry any combination; the fictional name is flavour. Agree the tags on each piece before deployment.

TagEffect
Obscuring+1 Evasion against attacks targeting units benefiting from it.
Cover+1 Armor.
DifficultCosts double movement.
BlockingFully blocks line of sight through it.
DangerousEach model entering or moving through rolls d10; 4+ passes. Each failure inflicts 1 wound.
ImpassableGround units cannot move through at all.

A ruin is Obscuring + Cover + Difficult + Blocking. A sandbag line is Obscuring + Cover. A minefield is Dangerous.

Blast, Engulf, and template weapons ignore cover entirely — their targets cannot benefit from it.

07

Type & Category

Every unit carries two independent labels, and both carry rules.

  • TYPE — what the unit is. Six: Infantry, Cavalry, Vehicle, Monster, Aerial, Towable.
  • CATEGORY — its battlefield role and Platoon slot. Six: ARMOR, COMMAND, LINE, RECON, SHOCK, SUPPORT.

The two are fully orthogonal. A COMMAND unit can be any TYPE. There is no separate "Hero" role — leadership is expressed through the COMMAND category.

Category rules

Impact(X) and Fear from earlier drafts are gone, replaced by Run Them Through and Terrifying respectively.

09

Missions & Combined Arms

Combined arms is a primary design goal, not a theme. Category caps shape what a list contains; the three rules below make a mono-arm list lose.

1 · Objectives need boots

TypeObjectives
Infantry, Cavalry, TowableMay claim. Costs an action — except LINE units, which claim for free.
Vehicle, Monster, AerialMay contest only. They deny an objective but can never score it.

A tank can park on a marker and stop you scoring it forever; it can never score it itself. Every army therefore needs infantry regardless of doctrine.

2 · Terrain density is a rule, not a suggestion

A standard 4'×4' board carries at least 8 terrain pieces, of which 3 or more carry Difficult or Impassable, and 4 or more carry Blocking. A board failing this is not a legal board — sparse terrain silently converts the game into a shooting contest.

3 · Anti-armor must actually kill armor

Dedicated anti-armor weapons need Damage ≥ 6 against Toughness 10 chassis. Massed armor should be a trap, punished by a comparatively cheap specialist.

The intended shape

InfantryTakes and holds ground. Only it scores.
ArmorBreaks through and denies, but cannot hold.
ArtilleryKills at range, blind without RECON, helpless up close.
ReconSees for the artillery and cannot fight.
AerialStrikes anywhere and holds nothing.
10

Transports

Transport(X) where X is a model capacity, not a unit count. Eligible cargo is Infantry and Cavalry.

Embarking and disembarking are both part of a Move action, costing half the unit's Speed (round up). While embarked a unit does not claim or contest objectives, and all measurement is taken from the Transport.

Dedicated Transports

A Transport attached to one specific unit at list building takes the CATEGORY of the unit it carries and consumes no additional slot. It must deploy carrying that unit, and still counts as its own activation.

Category inheritance is what keeps this honest. A slot-free transport with no category would be a free unit with no downside; instead it is a fully exposed member of the formation it serves. Choosing what a vehicle carries chooses what it is vulnerable to — a gunboat full of LINE infantry is hunted by Anti-Line, while a pure tank in the ARMOR slot is hunted by Anti-Armor.

Hull classes

Capacity and firepower compete for the same hull. Which class a faction favours is its armor doctrine.

ClassCapacityArmamentRole
Bus12–14Token — one light weaponMoves mass cheaply. Delivers and leaves.
Gunboat6–8A main gun plus secondariesFights and carries. Stays and contributes.
Assault Transport5–6Heavy, short-rangedSurvives the approach and disgorges into melee.

A faction whose transports fight does not need many pure tanks — its armor doctrine arrives in the transport slot instead of the ARMOR slot. That is how a faction capped at ARMOR 0–1 still fields serious vehicle firepower, and it couples the arms physically.

Fire or deliver — the IFV/APC split. A Transport with token armament (Bus) must choose between firing its weapon and ordering a disembark in the same activation — the gun isn't worth much, so its real job that turn is one or the other. A Transport with real armament (Gunboat, Assault Transport) may do both — the gun is good enough to be doing real work regardless of what its cargo is doing. This is a property of the hull, not of what it's carrying: any unit riding a Gunboat gets the benefit, not only Dragoons (below).

Open- and Closed-topped

Open-topped cargo may still act, measuring from the Transport, but is independently targetable using its own Evasion and Armor and counts as in Cover. Closed-topped cargo cannot act or be targeted.

Bailout

When a Transport is removed from play, every embarked model takes a Mettle check; each failure inflicts 1 wound. Survivors are placed within 3" of the wreck. If the Transport was Aerial and the cargo is not, the cargo is destroyed outright.

Step 1 · Model cost

  • E is 1 ÷ P(hit), normalised so Evasion 6 = 1.00.
  • A is 1 ÷ P(damage) vs a reference AP 1 attack, normalised so Armor 4 = 1.00.
  • S (Speed) = 1 + (Speed − 5) × 0.06
  • M (Mettle) = 1 + (Mettle − 4) × 0.06

Why the terms multiply rather than add. Being good at everything has to cost superlinearly, or elite units are undercosted by construction. An Oathkeeper pays not for durability plus mobility but durability × mobility — 3.8× a Guardsman per model, before weapons. Most units cannot afford to be fast and armored and tough, and that is the intended pressure: for an elite unit, the cost is the downside.

Step 2 · Weapon cost

Weapons are priced against two reference targets, because a single one badly misprices anti-tank guns.

Soft target — EVA 6 / ARM 4 Hard target — EVA 5 / ARM 8 Soft = Attacks × 0.5 × P_soft × min(Damage, 2) Hard = Attacks × 0.6 × P_hard × Damage Value = (Soft + Hard) ÷ 2 Weapon Cost = 7.09 × Value × R × (1 + Attacks × AP × 0.03)
  • min(Damage, 2) is an overkill cap — a Damage 10 shell is no better than a Damage 2 one against a Toughness 1 rifleman. Without it, every anti-tank weapon prices as though it were also the best anti-infantry weapon in the game.
  • The final term is the penetration × volume premium. A weapon that is both piercing and high-volume is the strongest thing on the table.
  • Melee weapons subtract the value of an equivalent unarmed attack, since that baseline is free (§04).

Range multiplier R = 0.6 + Range ÷ 30, with melee at 0.85.

Weapon traits

Every trait is multiplicative — value scales with what the weapon already does, so cost should too. Rather than individually arguing ~15 trait values, they're grouped into two bonus tiers and two restriction tiers, each one fixed multiplier. Bonus tier: does the trait change the shape of the attack (major), or just improve the odds on an otherwise-normal shot (minor)? Restriction tier: does it narrow what can be targeted (minor), or when the unit can act at all (major) — Heavy sits alone in the major tier, since "can't move and shoot" is a different category of cost than "can only shoot in one direction."

Tier×Traits
Major bonus1.30Linked-Weapon, Blast (L), Engulf (L), Guided, Indirect, Overcharge
Minor bonus1.10Accurate, Blast (S), Engulf (S), Suppressing, Turret, Pistol
Minor restriction0.90Coaxial, Frontal/Rear/Side Arc
Major restriction0.75Heavy
Anti-[Keyword] (each, stacks)1.20—

Multiple traits compound, they don't add. A weapon with traits A and B costs base × A × B, not base × (1 + (A−1) + (B−1)). Deliberate, documented here because the two diverge fast: 2 major bonuses stacked is a 5.6% gap between compounding and adding, 3 is 15.6%, a hypothetical 5-trait weapon is 48.5%. At the trait counts actually on the roster (2–3) the gap barely registers — it only bites on a weapon someone loads up with everything.

Compounding is derived, not merely chosen, for model stats — durability really is Toughness ÷ (P_hit × P_dmg), a genuine multiplicative relationship. Weapon traits have no such derivation; the tier multipliers are hand-picked either way. So this is a free design choice, made for consistency with that same instinct: the weapon tiers are already about specialization (Obliterator, Automatic, and Special are deliberately single-purpose, not swiss-army guns), and compounding actively discourages stacking many bonus traits onto one weapon — reinforcing that theme rather than fighting it.

Step 3 · Unit size scaling

In alternating activation the scarce resource is the activation, not the model. Sixteen bodies delivering one activation are worth less per model than five delivering one. Overkill waste and coherency drag push the same way.

Degradation is not the justification, despite being the intuitive one. Average output over a unit's lifetime is (N+1)/2N, which collapses immediately and then flattens — 55% at ten models, 53% at sixteen. It cannot explain a discount between those two sizes.

Step 4 · Faction signature-trait multipliers

One per faction, applied to the whole unit total (see §14). Individually hand-priced rather than tiered — there are only five of them in the whole game, so there's little pressure to systematize the way the 15 weapon traits needed to be.

Step 5 · Special/named unit abilities — flat, not multiplicative

Priority Orders, Leadership Abilities, Triggered effects. These get a flat point cost, on principle: a weapon trait multiplies because it modifies a base the weapon already has, and a stat multiplies because durability is a genuine multiplicative relationship. A standalone ability like "ignore Shaken entirely" doesn't modify either kind of base — it's a capability, not a modifier — and its value has essentially nothing to do with how many guns the unit is carrying. Charging a % of the unit's grand total would make the same ability cost more on a unit that spent its points on weapons than one that spent them on chassis, which has nothing to do with what the ability does.

TierCostUse for
Major15Ignoring a whole state (Shaken, Suppressing), a strong once-per-game reroll, a wide-reaching aura
Minor5A narrow or conditional edge — situational, small in scope

3× ratio, matching the bonus-trait tier gap (1.10 vs 1.30). These sit outside the size-scaling curve — they usually attach to one model (a leader's Priority Order), not the whole squad, so they shouldn't get cheaper just because the squad is large.

Other surcharges: Transport(X) = X × 0.439.

Weakest layer Every trait multiplier and ability tier is a hand-assigned guess. Everything above them in the formula derives from probability. This is where balance will break first.

The currency scale

BASE_MODEL and BASE_WEAPON move together. Multiplying both by the same factor rescales every cost proportionally and changes no relative balance whatsoever — it is a pure unit conversion. They are calibrated so the anchor Rifle Squad lands on exactly 100 points.

Trait Glossary

Targeting & templates

Blast (S/L)
May target a point instead of a model, centring a circular template. On a miss, scatter the point of impact 1d5" (S) or 1d10" (L) in the direction rolled. Targets cannot benefit from cover.
Engulf (S/L)
Uses a small or large teardrop template; targets cannot benefit from cover.
Indirect
May target an enemy without line of sight, provided an allied unit with Spotter has line of sight to that target.
Guided
If the target is visible to an allied RECON unit, decrease the target's Evasion by 3, disregarding all other modifiers.
Accurate
Ignore all range penalties.
Optics Stub
"Helps land hits" — mechanism not yet written. Currently costs nothing in the points formula; do not treat a weapon carrying it as finalized.

Firing restrictions

Heavy
Cannot attack in the same activation its unit moved; if it attacks first, it cannot then move.
Hardpoints
Ignore the Heavy trait.
Pistol
May make a ranged attack while engaged, but only at an enemy unit it is engaged with.
Turret
May fire from any facing.
Frontal / Rear / Side Arc
May only target models in that facing.
Coaxial
Must target the same target as the weapon named in the annotation.
Linked-Weapon
Re-roll all misses.
Fixed
Cannot be removed, swapped, or exchanged for an upgrade. Used for equipment that comes as part of another weapon, such as a Bayonet on a Rifle.

Damage & suppression

Suppressing
Targets gain a suppression marker regardless of the attack's outcome.
Overcharge
This weapon may fire in Overcharged mode: its AP and Damage are each increased by 2 for that attack. For each unmodified roll of 1 made for this weapon's attack, the bearer suffers a Damage 1 hit that cannot be saved against, in addition to any other effect of that roll.
Anti-[Keyword]
Against a target with the matching keyword, each successful damage roll counts as two hits instead of one. Because hits are allocated individually, the extra hits may spill onto other models in the unit. Defined for every TYPE and CATEGORY.
Bulwark
Reduce incoming damage by 1, to a minimum of 1.

Durability & morale

Regeneration
At the end of every round, regain 1d5 lost wounds.
Fearless
Ignores suppression markers on Mettle checks, and ignores the negative effects of deteriorating morale unless Routing.
Entrenched
While in cover, +1 Armor and re-roll failed Mettle checks.
Camouflaged
+1 Evasion while in cover.
All-Terrain
Ignores difficult terrain penalties.

Cost-modifying

Conscript ×0.75
This unit costs 25% less. It may never re-roll a die for any reason, and may not take the Rally action. Conscripts get raw numbers and none of the benefits of training: Entrenched's Mettle re-roll and Massed Ranks' to-hit re-roll both go dead, and once suppressed they can only clear markers one at a time by passing checks they are bad at.
Critical Weakspot Discount, not priced yet
When a Damage check against this model is rolled as an unmodified 10, the attacking weapon's Damage is doubled instead of applied normally. A model-level discount, priced like Conscript — a rare, high-drama vulnerability rather than a steady tax. First use: the Marten's extended-fuel-tank package.

Medical support

Combat Medic
Passive — while a friendly Infantry or Cavalry unit is within 6" of this model, a model in that unit that would be removed as a casualty instead makes a Mettle check; on a pass, it is not removed and is instead treated as having 1 wound remaining. Active — as an action, this model may restore 1d5 lost wounds to models in one friendly Infantry or Cavalry unit within 6" — the same rate as Regeneration, delivered externally. Deliberately Infantry/Cavalry only; vehicle repair is a different, not-yet-designed mechanic.

Faction Identity

One signature trait per faction. Everything else — weapon list, equipment, category caps, doctrine — exists to reinforce that single idea.

The formula prices stats from real probability curves, so stat divergence is nearly free. Traits are hand-priced guesses. Be generous with stats, stingy with traits.

Dragoon — Rapid Deployment

Historical dragoons were named for the firearm they carried, not the horse — the horse was only ever the delivery system. That's the whole identity: a unit defined by its gun, whose mobility exists to get that gun into position faster than the enemy can react, not to win a melee. Modern shoot-and-scoot doctrine (originally a self-propelled artillery term) adds the other half — the actual resource being spent every activation is exposure time, not toughness or numbers.

Rapid Deployment — When the friendly Transport carrying this unit activates, it may order this unit to disembark as part of that activation, subject to the hull's fire-or-deliver restriction above. This unit disembarks immediately, at no cost to its own Move stat and without spending either of its own actions. When this unit is later activated, both of its actions are available as normal.

This unit may embark in or disembark from any friendly Transport, not a specific chassis — the mechanized identity isn't tied to one hull.

This unit may never be equipped with a weapon that has the Heavy trait. Hitting hard and fast is the whole point; a weapon that can't fire in the same activation it moved is the opposite of that.

Deliberately not gated behind a Mettle check or a resource cost. The balance is structural, not probabilistic: the unit is rented from the Transport, not owned outright, and the player is paying for two units — the Dragoons and whatever's carrying them — to get one effect. Kill the Transport and Rapid Deployment is gone for the rest of the game, no dice roll required to make that sting.

The premise: magic in this world is sworn, not cast. An oath kept channels power; an oath broken inverts into something worse. The Sworn Empire is not a state that uses oath-magic — it is the oath-network, and citizenship is a contract position. "Chaos" is the breaking of the First Binding that holds the plane itself, which collapses every scale of oathbreaking into one act: a deserter and a daemon are the same event at different magnitudes.

Open Threads

Genuinely open as of the last working session — most of the original v0.3 list (Mettle direction, who rolls damage, the AP scale, Transport/Indirect/Smoke rule text) has since been resolved and dropped from this list.

  1. Trait-tier and ability-tier multipliers are still guesses. The four weapon-trait tiers, the five faction signature-trait multipliers, and the two ability tiers are all hand-picked. Everything else in the formula derives from probability. First thing playtesting should attack.
  2. Sapper, Hunter, and Dragoon have no stat lines. Sapper is additionally missing its actual terrain-clearing rule (Breach, conceptually settled, not yet written up formally) — the one piece that answers this document's own §09 terrain-density requirement.
  3. Optics (Marksman Rifle) is a rules stub — "helps land hits," no mechanism defined, priced at 0 until it is.
  4. ARMOR and SUPPORT generic Platoon Abilities are undesigned (the five faction-specific doctrines exist; the three generic Vanguard-Platoon abilities from §08 are Line/Shock/Recon only).
  5. Platoon slot constraints aren't encoded in the .gst — the proposed spread in §08 is documented but not enforced by the force entry itself.
  6. The AFV at 241 is 2.4 rifle squads — 24% of a 1000-point list. If centrepieces feel over-taxed, soften the size exponent from 0.85 toward 0.90.
  7. Squad upgrade options are specified but not generated — NCO, special weapons, embedded HWT, dedicated transport.
  8. Dice volume at the top end is untested. Two rolls per attack is fine at rifle scale; an Attacks 4 weapon across several models will find the ceiling.
  9. Alpha strike. A Damage 7–10 gun against Toughness 10 deletes the most expensive unit class in one hit.
  10. Facings have no general section. Armored Front and the arc traits imply one; none is written.
  11. No mission or scenario exists against the current rules.
  12. Conscripts have no melee weapon at all — a good differentiator that arrived by accident. Worth keeping on purpose.
  13. The toolchain is duplicated across the Praxis Belli project workspace and the PraxisBelli git repo (copied, not moved) — a real drift risk until one is picked as canonical.
